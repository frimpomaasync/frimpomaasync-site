/01

What you probably bought last time

If you have already spent five figures on this and regret it, I want to start there rather than pretend it did not happen.

There is a post that has been sitting on Reddit for a while now, from the owner of a fourteen-year-old agency. They took quotes from six firms to customise one of the big work-management platforms. The cheapest was fourteen thousand dollars. They went mid-range and paid twenty thousand.

It was quoted at one to two months. It took six. The links between the pieces kept silently breaking, which meant, in their words, that jobs got lost and forgotten. The fix their supplier offered was to migrate to a fresh account, which cost them about a month of data they then had to reconstruct by hand to work out what they were owed.

Source: r/productivity, 1,061 upvotes, 172 comments. I am not linking it to score a point off a competitor. I am linking it because it is the single most useful description of how these builds fail, and everything below is shaped around not doing that.

Underneath that story is the thing almost nobody selling automation will say out loud. Buying the system is the easy half. Somebody has to keep it running afterwards, and when that somebody is you, it quietly becomes another job you did not want. As one owner put it this year, tools and systems can help organise things, but someone still has to maintain them, or everything ends up back in your head again.

/02

Steal this first · free, no call needed

Before you spend anything with anyone, including me, run this on your own business. It takes an afternoon and it will tell you whether you need this at all.
The four-column audit

Open a blank sheet. Four columns: what happens, who touches it, where it is written down, what breaks if they leave. Now walk one customer from first contact to final payment and fill in a row for every single step, including the ones that only live in your head.

Two things fall out of this every time. First, you will find steps where the same information gets typed in twice, into two different systems, by two different people. That is where errors come from and it is almost always fixable in a day. Second, you will find rows where the fourth column just says one person's name. That is not an automation problem. That is a risk, and you should write those steps down this week whether or not you ever speak to me.

If the sheet comes back short and tidy, you do not need a twenty-five thousand dollar anything. Buy the Client Catcher instead, or nothing at all.

Questions worth asking before you spend this much

Who is this actually for?

A business doing enough volume that admin has become a real cost, usually somewhere past a million in revenue, with a handful of staff and four or five systems that do not talk to each other. If you are a solo owner with a quiet phone, this is the wrong product and the Client Catcher at fifteen hundred is the right one.

Why is it twenty-five thousand and not a monthly fee?

Because a monthly fee makes you my tenant. Small steady subscriptions are exactly what owners tell me they are sick of. You buy it once, you own it, and the ongoing cost is your own metered usage rather than rent to me.

How is this different from hiring somebody?

An office manager covering this work costs more than this in the first year, then again every year after, and takes the knowledge with them when they leave. This does not resign. It also does not have judgement, so it hands the genuinely hard calls to you rather than guessing.

What happens if my business changes?

It is built from parts and documented, so changing a step is editing a step. That is the point of handing over the instructions rather than keeping them. The care plan exists for owners who would rather I did the editing, but it is optional and always will be.

What do you need from me?

Roughly a day of your time in total, spread over the month. Access to your accounts, honest answers about how things really work rather than how they are supposed to, and one decision-maker who can approve things. That is it.

Do I have to buy the Client Catcher first?

No, but most people should. It is fifteen hundred, it is live in seven days, and it proves whether we work well together before you spend this much. If you then buy this, the fifteen hundred comes off the price.

Can I see it working before I pay?

Yes, and you should insist on it with anybody you are considering. Watch it work before you pay is not a slogan I put on the site to sound good. Book the call and I will show you the live one.
