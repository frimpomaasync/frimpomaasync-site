The email follow-up system

Write it once. It keeps working.

You already know the follow-up matters. The problem is that following up is a job, and you are the only person here. So write the six emails once and let them send themselves.

Six emails. That is the whole system.

Not a funnel, not a launch. Six messages that cover the moments where money quietly leaks out of a small business.

Sends immediately

The welcome.

Tells them what they just got, what happens next, and how to reach a real person. This one email prevents most of the confused replies you get today.

Day three

The useful one.

The answer to the question you get every single week. No pitch. This is where people decide whether you know your job.

After the quote

The nudge.

Short, specific, no apology. It asks whether the price or the timing is the problem, and it wins back the ones who simply forgot.

Day eight

The invoice reminder.

Friendly and factual. Written once, sent by a machine, so you never have to be the person chasing money on a Friday.

The evening after

The thanks and re-book.

Sent the day of the job while they still feel it. The cheapest booking you will ever get is the one from a client you already have.

Every so often

The quiet check-in.

For the regular who drifted. One paragraph, no guilt, one easy way back. Send it to twenty people and two will book.

Before the emails

You need somewhere to send them.

A list is the only audience you own. Every other place you post is rented, and the rent changes without warning. Starting one is smaller than it sounds.

1 · Pick one thing worth having. A checklist, a price guide, the page you keep sending people. 2 · Put it behind an email address. One field. Not a form with nine questions. 3 · Put the link in three places: your bio, your email signature, your voicemail greeting. 4 · Send the welcome email straight away, and the useful one three days later. 5 · After that, once a month is plenty. Consistency beats volume.

Twenty people who asked to hear from you is a better list than two thousand who did not.

What actually sends them.
