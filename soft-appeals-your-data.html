Three rules that never bend.

Everything else on this page is detail. These three are the ones that get a contract torn up if I break them.

One

The agreement comes first.

A signed business associate agreement, the kind HIPAA requires, before the first byte of patient information moves. No signed agreement, no claims, however friendly the conversation has been.

Two

Never ordinary email.

Not in the body, not as an attachment, not in the subject line. If claims arrive by regular email anyway, they get moved into covered storage and removed from the mailbox, and I tell you it happened.

Three

AI only under agreement.

AI drafts appeals here, and I say so openly. Every drafting tool that touches a claim runs inside a service that has signed its own business associate agreement covering patient data. Nothing about your patients goes into a consumer chatbot, and nothing about your patients trains anything.

The safest copy is the one that never existed

I would rather work inside your systems than hold your data.

Where your practice can issue me a login to your billing system or a payer portal, that is the first choice every time. Work happens there, under credentials you control and can switch off in a second. Nothing lands on my side, so nothing on my side needs protecting.

When a copy has to exist, it is the smallest one the appeal can be built from. Initials in working papers. Full names only where a payer's own form demands them. Claim references instead of patient names in calendars, file names and task lists.

What carries your data, and what never does.

A short list on purpose. Anything not on it does not carry patient information, and there is no exception for being in a hurry.

01

A secure upload link

How your denial exports reach me. You get the link after the agreement is signed. It is yours alone and it expires.

02

Covered mail and storage

Encrypted, access-logged, and under an agreement that covers patient data. Every account that touches it has a unique login and two-factor sign-in.

03

A healthcare fax line

Because payers still run on fax, and that is where proof of delivery comes from.

04

Your own systems

Your billing system and payer portals, under credentials you issue and can revoke.

05

Never these

Ordinary email, text messages, consumer chatbots, shared computers, calendar titles, file names. Not once, not for something small.

06

Never offshore

All work happens in the United States, under one accountable name. No overseas processing, no subcontracted labour pool, nobody you have not been told about.

I do not publish the names of the services behind these channels, for the same reason a practice does not publish its own vendor list. Every one of them is named in the agreement you sign, and I will walk you through all of them on the call.

If something goes wrong

You hear it from me within 72 hours.

Maryland gives a business in my position ten days to tell you. The agreement I sign says 72 hours, because ten days is long enough for a small problem to become your problem.

What you get is plain: what happened, which data, how many patients, what has already been done, and what happens next. Near-misses get written down too, and the root cause gets fixed inside 30 days with a note saying what changed. Quietly fixing something and not telling you is the one thing that turns a small error into a real violation.

When the work ends

Your data leaves with you.

At the end of an engagement, claim-level data is returned or destroyed, whichever you choose, on the terms in the agreement. What stays on my side is the business record: invoices, totals, dates. Nothing that identifies a patient.

While the work runs

Access granted, access removed, written down.

Every login you issue and every login you switch off gets a dated line in a log. If your practice is ever audited, or you simply want to know who could see what and when, the answer already exists and I do not have to reconstruct it.

Six questions to ask anyone who wants your claims.

Use this list on every vendor, including me. Every question here is answered somewhere on this page, in writing, and the agreement repeats it.

01

Will you sign a business associate agreement before any data moves?

The only acceptable answer is yes, before, in every case. Anyone who wants "a quick look first" has already told you how they handle patient data.

02

Where does the data live, and who can reach it?

You want named channels, unique logins, two-factor sign-in and an access log. "It's secure" is not an answer. A short list of specific places is.

03

Do your AI tools carry their own agreement?

If AI touches the claims, the service running it needs its own business associate agreement, and nothing may train on your patients. Ask for both in writing.

04

What is your breach notice window?

A number of hours, in the contract. Mine is 72. A vendor who cannot name a number is planning to decide later, under pressure, with their own interests in the room.

05

Is any of the work offshored or subcontracted?

You are entitled to know every person and country that touches your patients' information. Get the answer written into the agreement itself.

06

What happens to the data when we stop?

Returned or destroyed, your choice, on terms written down before you start. If that is missing from the contract, ask for it to be added before you sign.

Read the agreement before you decide anything.

Ask on the call and the business associate agreement comes to you as a document, to read on your own time and send to your own attorney. Nothing is signed in a meeting.

You get

The privacy agreement, in full.

The 72-hour promise, the return-or-destroy terms, the named services, and what happens if either of us walks away.

You get

The recovery agreement, separately.

Fees, which claims are in scope, which are excluded, and your approval step before anything is submitted in your practice's name.

You keep

The right to stop.

Switch off my access, ask for the data back, and the engagement ends. No notice period, no exit fee, no claim on work in progress.
