Behavioral health · ask me about this one

Did Carelon pay you interest on the 2025 late claims?

Through 2025 the ASO's claim inventory peaked at 437,102 in March, and claims aged past thirty days peaked at 138,081 in April. If you billed the public behavioral health system last year, you were almost certainly paid late on a meaningful number of claims.

Maryland's prompt payment law reaches the behavioral health ASO. It sits in Health-General § 15-103(b)(21)(vi), not in the Insurance Article and not in any provider manual, which is why almost nobody knows it is there. It requires interest at 1.5% per month on days 31 to 60, 2% on days 61 to 120, and 2.5% after that. And § 15-1005(g)(2) says that interest is supposed to be included in the payment without the provider ever asking for it.

So the only real question is whether it was. Most practices have never checked, because nobody told them there was anything to check. I will check it for free. If you were paid correctly, I will tell you that and we are done.

Process

    What came back, matched against what was owed, including statutory interest where it applies.

Some of what your carrier is unlikely to volunteer.

180 daysstatutory floor
You have at least 180 days to file a claim. This is a statutory floor and it overrides a shorter window written into your participation agreement. Practices write off timely-filing denials at 90 days because that is what the contract says. The contract loses.
Md. Ins. § 15-1005(e)(1)Timely filing
1.5 / 2 / 2.5%per month
Interest per month on late payment. Days 31 to 60, days 61 to 120, and beyond. And it must be included without you claiming it.
Md. Ins. § 15-1005(g)Prompt payment interest
30 / 45working days
Working days, not calendar days. That is what an internal grievance decision runs on. Forty-five working days is roughly nine calendar weeks.
Md. Ins. § 15-10A-02Internal grievance

To take a complaint to the Insurance Commissioner. In Maryland the regulator conducts the external review itself.
Md. Ins. § 15-10A-03(a)(1)External review

Recoupment lookback. The improper-coding exception fails entirely unless the carrier gave you its coding guidelines at least 30 days before the date of service.
