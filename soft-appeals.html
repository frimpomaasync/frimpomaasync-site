Questions practices ask before they send the pile.

Q1

Will my biller feel replaced?

No. I only touch claims that already bounced and were written off. Submissions, coding, posting: all still theirs. Most billers are glad to see the pile leave, and the ones I work with send me their overflow.

Q2

Is patient data safe with you?

A signed privacy agreement, the kind HIPAA requires, comes before any claim moves. Patient data never travels through ordinary email or an open chatbot, and every tool that touches it carries its own agreement. US-based, never offshored. The whole answer is on its own page, including what happens if something ever goes wrong.

Q3

How do I know what you recovered?

You see it before I do. The payer pays your account and sends you the remittance paperwork. My invoice references that paperwork, line by line.

Q4

Are all denials worth fighting?

No, and knowing which to decline is half the skill. Dead claims are named in the audit and left alone. Protecting the win rate protects your money and my time.

Q5

What if the claims are too old?

Appeal windows run 30 to 180 days from the denial. Some claims in your pile die this month whether anyone acts or not. That is why the audit flags expiring claims first, and why waiting is the one real cost.

Q6

Does anything go out without me seeing it?

Never. You approve every appeal before it is submitted in your practice's name. If a claim looks like it needs a coding change, it comes back to you flagged for someone qualified to decide, and I do not touch it.

Q7

Why is the audit free?

Because it is the proof. If the number on that one page does not make the decision for you, nothing said on a sales call would either. Watch it work before you pay.
