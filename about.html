I built this because hospitals had it and small businesses did not.

Before this I worked in hospitals. I started at the front desk, checking patients in. Then a bachelor's, then a master's, and along the way HR, building the forms the hospital ran on, and performance improvement for a healthcare system. I did SQL and data management, and I helped hospital systems go live with Epic.

What I kept noticing was the gap. Everything those organisations had, small businesses did not. The scheduling, the tracking, the follow-up that runs quietly in the background of a hospital, none of it reached the person running a business on their own. That is a real disadvantage, and nobody was fixing it.

I run a one-person business too. So I build those systems for people like me, in accounts they own, with no subscription to keep paying for, and simple enough to run yourself. Your customer gets the experience of dealing with a big company, from a business of one.

How I communicate

A written update every Friday, whether or not there is news.

What moved, what is next, what I am waiting on from you, and whether the launch date has changed. Going quiet is how this work usually goes wrong, so it is the one habit I do not skip. Weekdays, I answer inside four hours.

How decisions get made

In writing, before the work, not after the argument.

The scope is a document you sign. A change to it is priced in writing before I start it. If you ask me something commercial on a call and I do not have a good answer, I will say I am sending it that afternoon instead of improvising a number.

What I will not do. Worth knowing before you book anything.

Turning work down is cheaper for both of us than taking it and getting it wrong.
