Four things to check when it arrives.

Hold your report against these. Hold anyone else's against them too.

01

Are the dollars allowed, or billed?

Billed charges run two to four times the contracted rate. A report built on billed numbers looks bigger and means nothing, because a won appeal only ever pays the contract. Every figure in this report is the allowed amount.

02

Is there a dead pile at all?

A report where everything is winnable is a sales document. Roughly a third of the sample above is named as not worth fighting, with the reason. Declining claims is part of the work.

03

Are the deadlines real dates?

Not "act fast", not "limited time". A payer's published appeal window, counted from the date on your remittance, landing on a named day. You can check every one of them yourself.

04

Can you see the working?

Behind the one page sits a row per claim: the code, the payer's own claim number, the remittance date, the bucket, the deadline. Ask for it and it comes. A number you cannot trace is a number you should not trust.

What the report is not

Nobody can promise you a recovery.

The winnable figure is an estimate built from published overturn rates by denial type. It is the honest expectation on that pile, not a number anyone owes you. Payers can and do uphold denials that deserved to be overturned.

What is promised is smaller and firmer. The audit costs nothing. The report is yours whatever you decide. Nothing is submitted in your practice's name without your approval. And if a claim recovers nothing, it costs you nothing.
